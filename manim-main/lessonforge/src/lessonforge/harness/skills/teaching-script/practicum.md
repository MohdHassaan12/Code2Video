# The try-it-yourself task

Most classrooms have no idea ready for a practical on a given topic. Every lesson
carries one small task the learner does by hand, and a version of it for a teacher with
a full class and five minutes.

A video watched alone leaves a feeling of understanding that is often false. A short
task that makes the learner use the idea is what tests it (Hörnlein & Kulgemeyer 2026).
Handling objects alone teaches little: the learning is in the prediction before and the
explanation after (Abrahams & Millar 2008; Crouch et al. 2004).

## Rules for the task

1. **After the explanation, never before.** The learner needs the idea to know what to
   look at.
2. **Things already at home or in a school bag.** Paper, squared paper, pencil, ruler,
   scissors, thread, coins, beans or seeds, buttons, rubber band, a glass, water, a
   bottle, a ball, a book, a torch or phone light, a mirror, salt, sugar, a straw, a
   balloon. Nothing that burns, cuts skin, is swallowed, or uses mains electricity.
3. **Five steps or fewer**, each one action.
4. **Say what each object stands for**, in one line ("each bean is one unit; a row is
   one side of the square"). Without it the objects are just objects.
5. **Predict first.** One question with a definite answer the learner commits to
   before touching anything.
6. **Say what they should see.** State the true outcome plainly. Learners often record
   what they expected instead of what happened.
7. **Ask why.** The question after the task asks for the reason, and the stored answer
   carries the reason ("… because the two has no partner").
8. **End in words.** The learner writes or says two lines. Do not ask for a drawing
   after an animated lesson.
9. **Choose the kind honestly.** `hands` when the learner can really produce the
   effect with these things. `paper` when the task is done on paper with numbers or a
   drawing frame. `simulation` only when the thing cannot be seen or done safely; then
   say what to look for in the lesson's own animation.

## The class version

The same task for 40 students and five minutes: who works with whom (pairs), what the
teacher writes on the board, the one question the teacher asks at the end, and what a
wrong answer from the class would tell the teacher.

## In the narration

The lesson sets the task in two or three spoken sentences at the end of practice: what
to take, what to predict, the question to answer. It does not give the answer.
