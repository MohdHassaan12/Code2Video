# The three moves of a step

A teacher at a board does three things with every step, in this order. A beat of the
script is one step done this way.

**Write.** The thing appears as it is said: a line is drawn, a number is written, a part
moves. The sentence talks about exactly that.

**Explain.** Say why this step is allowed or why it comes out this way. Use "because".
For the one or two steps that carry the lesson, ask it first ("why are we allowed to
split them like this?"), give thinking time, then answer.

**Elaborate.** One link, then move on. Choose the one that helps most here:
- a contrast case that comes out differently ("now try the same with 18"),
- a link back to the picture ("that is the side of our square"),
- a "what if" that changes one thing,
- a check the learner can run for themselves.

## One step, written dull and written well

Dull (the rule is announced, nothing is explained, nothing is linked):

> Prime factors ko identical pairs mein group kijiye. Agar har factor pair mein hai,
> toh number square number hai.

Well (write, explain with a question, elaborate with a contrast):

> Thirty-six ke prime factors likhiye: two, two, three, three. Ab inhe do barabar
> hisson mein baantiye, ek two aur ek three idhar, ek two aur ek three udhar. Dono
> taraf six bana. Yeh kyun hua? Because six into six mein har prime do baar aata hai,
> ek baar pehle six se, ek baar doosre six se. Ab eighteen ke saath wahi kijiye: two,
> three, three. Two ka koi partner nahi hai, isliye do barabar hisse ban hi nahi sakte.

The second version is longer by a few seconds and it is the only one from which a
learner can say why.

## Order inside a lesson

- A case before the rule, and two cases side by side when the rule is a test (one that
  passes, one that fails).
- The opening question is asked in the first two sentences and answered by name in the
  stage that works the full example.
- The stage that names the idea also says the WHY from the card, in one beat, while
  the picture shows it.
- Where practice ends, set the try-it task in two or three sentences, in this order:
  what to take, the guess to make first, the question to answer afterwards. The lesson
  never says the task's result or its reason, and the practice just before it uses a
  different case, so the task is not solved for the learner. Do not mention an app or
  a card: the sentences must be enough for someone who only hears them.
- The mistake is met by doing it: say it as a learner would, carry it out on one case,
  and let the result fail on screen. Naming the mistake in a sentence is a warning,
  and a warning is forgotten.
- One picture, continued. Each chapter starts from what the last one left on screen:
  the same objects in the same places, changed one step further.
