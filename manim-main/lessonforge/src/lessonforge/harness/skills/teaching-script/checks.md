# The reviewer's questions

Each is answered yes or no, and a "yes" must be shown by quoting the beat. A reviewer
is reliable when asked whether something is there, and unreliable when asked whether a
lesson is "engaging" (docs/research/approaches/04, evaluation). So every question here
is about something that can be pointed at.

1. Do the first two sentences ask a question about the big idea and ask for a guess?
2. Is that question answered by name later, and not in the sentence after it?
3. Is the card's WHY said in one beat, with "because" or an equivalent, while a
   picture shows it?
4. Is that WHY first put to the learner as a question?
5. Does every beat have something on screen that its sentences talk about?
6. Is the same picture carried through the lesson and changed, instead of replaced?
7. Are two cases compared side by side before the rule is stated?
8. Is one of the card's mistakes stated as a learner would make it, and shown to fail?
9. Do the wrong choices in the questions come from the card's mistakes?
10. Does at least one question ask the learner to predict or decide, not to recall?
11. Is the try-it task set in the narration, with what to take, what to predict and
    the question, and without its answer?
12. Does any chapter open with a definition, or carry the stage's name as its title?
    (A "yes" here is a fault.)
13. Does anything in the script contradict a passage of the sources?
