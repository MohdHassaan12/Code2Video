No end-to-end quality score

The system can prove a video is geometrically clean. It cannot say whether it is any good. Until quality can be scored, every other lever on this site is tuned on eyeballs — which is why this problem ranks first.

⚙️ Mechanics today — what IS measured

Verification is real and layered, but every layer measures geometry or plumbing:

  • The dry-run probe (_OVERLAP_PROBE_SNIPPET, app/render.py) patches Scene.play and counts, after every beat: text-on-text overlap, clipped text at the frame edge, text-over-shape.
  • Admission gates on the storyboard check truncation, shape, and on-screen language (storyboard_shape_problems, app/storyboard.py:32) — never content quality.
  • Every video ships a sidecar (_write_video_sidecar, app/jobs.py:67) with repair_rounds, violations_remaining, animation_beats, voice_degraded — all structural counters.
  • Per-stage grades are earned from runs (reference-architecture.md:850-851), and the harness battery (253/253 checks green, measured 2026-07-27) pins every layer’s contract. Nothing pins its quality.
🕳️ The gap — why this is research-grade

The roadmap says it outright: “no narration QA at all; no fidelity-to-source QA” pipeline-roadmap.md:510-511. A video with zero violations can still mumble, pace wrongly, explain badly, or teach nothing — and the pipeline will stamp it Clean on /timeline.

The consequence is systemic: storyboard prompt changes, Visual Director directives, entrance vocabulary, pacing, the tech_terms spoken-language policy — all are currently judged by watching videos. The repo’s own discipline (“grades earned from evidence, never guessed”) stops exactly at the point where the evidence would need to be a quality score. What makes it hard: “good educational video” has no agreed computable definition; LLM-judge scores are cheap but drift, and the true metric — did a student learn — is unobservable at build time.

📎 Evidence
  • Stage G graded L2.5 — the grade text covers only the geometric probe family pipeline-roadmap.md:506.
  • Director live runs were assessed by reading its logs (“40 rescaled, 0 hides, 14 highlights, 3 rejected”) and watching the output — no score existed to compare against pipeline-roadmap.md:327-328.
  • The end-to-end benchmark is three topics chosen for historical breakage, evaluated by eye reference-architecture.md:1409-1411.
  • The bulk runner — the natural eval harness — is fully built (454 lines, bulk_runner.py) and has never produced a run: bulk_runs/ does not exist on disk (measured 2026-07-27).
📚 Prior art citation-check before external use

Rubric-based LLM-as-judge evaluation is standard practice for generated text (MT-Bench / AlpacaEval style), with known failure modes — position bias, verbosity bias, self-preference — that calibration against human ratings partially controls. Multimedia-learning research (Mayer’s principles: coherence, signaling, segmenting, modality) offers a defensible rubric source for instructional video. Generic video metrics (FVD-style distributional scores) measure realism, not pedagogy, and are a poor fit here. No widely accepted automated metric for “teaching quality of a generated explainer video” exists — that end of this problem is genuinely open.

🪜 Solution sketch — cheap → deep
  1. $0 Write the rubric as a doc (dimensions: correctness, coherence, pacing, visual-narration match, language contract) and add scored fields to the sidecar schema (schemas/render-verification-ir.schema.json + --regen, drift-gated by test_ir_contracts.py).
  2. metered An LLM-judge pass over storyboard + narration transcript per rubric dimension; calibrate once against human ratings of ~20 existing videos (25 mp4s already sit in static/videos/). Follow the house metric rule: check any new score reads sane on a known-good run first — the voice_degraded false-positive incident is the cautionary tale CLAUDE.md:159-164.
  3. metered Stand up the built-but-never-run bulk runner as the eval loop: a fixed topic suite, scored sidecars, per-stage score trends across commits — turning “grades earned from evidence” into a continuously earned number.
  4. research Validate: do rubric scores predict human preference? Do they predict comprehension (even a small quiz-based study)? This is the T3 tail — a proxy metric that tracks learning.
📏 How we’d measure it

Agreement with blinded human ranking on held-out videos; score stability across reruns of the same board; and a regression sensitivity test in the house style — inject a known-bad change (the probe suite already uses a forced_bug fixture) and require the score to drop. A quality score that never moves, or always moves, fails the repo’s own “a signal that always fires is no signal” rule.
