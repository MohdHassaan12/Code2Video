Trustworthy machine perception

Vision QA — the system looking at its own pixels — is built, wired, and tested. It stays default-off because its judgment cannot yet be trusted with the keys: making a vision model a reliable gate is an open problem.

⚙️ Mechanics today
  • VisionQAStage.critique (app/pipeline/vision_qa.py): ffmpeg extracts VISION_QA_FRAMES=6 frames, one vision-LLM call critiques them, and findings are whitelist- and shape-gated before they’re allowed to exist — emitted as LAYOUT_VIOLATION: vision-* lines that the geometric matcher is explicitly forbidden to count.
  • _run_vision_qa (app/render.py:726) runs report-only on the parallel render path (render.py:1152) and with accept-if-better repair on the sequential path (render.py:1247), under the same non-regression guard as overlap repair: the incumbent video is never lost.
  • Gated VISION_QA=0 by default (vision_qa.py:131); 35/35 harness checks green (test_vision_qa.py, measured 2026-07-27) — the plumbing is verified, the judgment is not.
🕳️ The gap — why this is research-grade

Two live runs showed the two canonical failure modes of machine perception as a gate:

  • It sees things that aren’t there. On the refraction board, all 6 sampled frames were flagged “dead frame” — but the sampler was landing on scene starts, when elements haven’t entered yet: “substantially a sampler artifact, not a composition defect” pipeline-roadmap.md:352-363.
  • Acting on what it sees can buy nothing. One vision round “spent 4 LLM rewrites + 4 scene re-renders + 1 re-critique and bought nothing on this board” pipeline-roadmap.md:566-568.

The deep issue: unlike the geometric probe — whose measurements are exact by construction — a vision critique has no ground truth, no calibration, and no cost model. The repo’s response (whitelists, shape gates, non-regression guards) is correct defensive engineering, but it caps the stage at “advisor.” Turning perception into a gate that can veto a publish — with a false-positive rate near zero, per the house rule that a signal that always fires is no signal — is the open problem, and nobody in the field has a reliable recipe for it yet.

📎 Evidence

  • Measured on disk: vision_findings present in 3 of 17 sidecars (values 3, 4, 6); the refraction sidecar records repair_rounds: 4, violations_remaining: 3, vision_findings: 6.

📚 Prior art citation-check before external use

VLM-based UI/screenshot critique is an active research area (design-to-code and web-agent evaluation work); known VLM weaknesses on dense text, small glyphs and precise spatial relations are well documented. Keyframe selection is a classic video-summarization problem — sampling at scene midpoints is the trivial fix, content-aware sampling the mature one. Ensembling and self-consistency reduce single-call variance at multiplied cost. Calibrated abstention (“know when you don’t know”) for VLM judges remains open — which is exactly the capability a publish-veto needs.

🪜 Solution sketch — cheap → deep
  1. $0 Sample at scene midpoints (the roadmap’s own named fix); derive timestamps from the per-scene clip boundaries the parallel renderer already knows.
  2. $0 Build a labeled golden-frame set from the 25 mp4s already on disk (good / dead / cluttered), so perception finally has a ground truth to be scored against.
  3. metered Ensemble: N independent vision calls vote per finding; measure precision/recall against the golden set; publish the stage’s own report card in the sidecar.
  4. research Calibrated veto: a confidence-thresholded gate whose false-positive rate on known-clean videos is measured ≈0 before it is ever allowed to block a publish — the standard the voice_degraded incident set for every new health signal.
📏 How we’d measure it

Precision/recall on the golden-frame set; zero findings on known-clean videos; and repair yield — of the re-renders a vision round triggers, what fraction reduce human-judged defects? The harness seam already exists (test_vision_qa.py stubs the vision LLM; the golden set gives the stub real answers).
