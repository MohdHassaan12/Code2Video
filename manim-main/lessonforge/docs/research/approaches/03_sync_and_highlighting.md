# 03 — Audio/visual sync and highlighting: how others do it

Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: narrated, auto-generated Manim lessons (English + Hindi/Hinglish). Everything below is from sources read during this pass; numbers are quoted from those sources.

Our baseline, for reference: narration synthesized first (Gemini TTS via OpenRouter, per teaching block), sentence boundaries snapped to pauses, word timestamps from OpenRouter `whisper-large-v3`, scene code waits for an anchor phrase ("cue"). Highlighting: each step appears on its phrase, earlier steps dim to 42 %, the key step gets a gold surrounding box.

### A.2 Adopt first

**1. Replace ASR timestamps with transcript-constrained forced alignment, behind an adapter.**
We already know the script, so timing it by re-transcribing is throwing away information; every benchmark puts WhisperX-style timing at 2–5× the error of a forced aligner and with a fat tail. Design:

- `align(audio_path, script_text, lang) -> [{word, start, end, score}]` with pluggable backends: **MFA 3.0** for `en` (mean ≈20 ms); **torchaudio MMS_FA + uroman** for `hi`/Hinglish (language-agnostic, romanize both Devanagari and Latin tokens; spell out numerals before alignment because uroman drops them); **ElevenLabs forced-alignment API** as the cloud fallback (Hindi listed; returns word `loss`).
- Gate: if a word's score is poor or the aligner drops it, interpolate linearly between neighbours (manim-voiceover's fallback) and mark the cue `low_confidence`; if > 10 % of a block's cue words are low-confidence, re-synthesize the block with pause tags around the anchor phrase.
- Pin the alignment to the *final muxed* audio, not the raw TTS clip, so trims/padding cannot shift it.

**2. Make script-anchored cue tokens the only timing primitive; resolve them to a cue table.**
Synthesia/HeyGen, Remotion and manim-voiceover all converge on "a cue is a position in the text". Design:

- Narration markup `… the <cue id="lhs">left-hand side</cue> equals …`. Cue onset = alignment start of the first word inside the tag; cue end = end of the last word.
- Build `cues.json` per block: `{id, t_on, t_off, words, confidence}`. Scene code calls `self.at("lhs")` → `safe_wait(t_on − lead − renderer.time)`; it never hard-codes seconds.
- **Timing rule**: visual onset = `t_on − lead`, `lead = 250 ms` (saccade ≈200 ms; picture-first lands inside the tolerated −125/−185 ms window far more often than audio-first). Clamp so a cue never fires before the previous cue's animation has had ≥ 300 ms on screen.
- `run_time` for the animation that introduces a referent = `clamp(t_off − t_on, 0.4 s, 1.5 s)`; longer spans are filled by a hold, not by slowing the animation.

**3. Audio-master contract with a render-time verifier.**
manim-voiceover's `voiceover()` guarantees audio-master by padding (`wait_for_voiceover`) but silently freezes the picture when visuals are short and delays the next audio when visuals are long. Design:

- Per block: `visual_span ∈ [0.6, 1.0] × speech_span`; violations fail generation (not render), and the fix is to insert `[short pause]`/`[medium pause]` Gemini markup around the anchor phrase or to split the beat, then re-synthesize and re-align.
- Scene logs `renderer.time` at every `at()` and at every mobject add/remove. After render, run the same aligner on the muxed output and assert `|t_visual − (t_on − lead)| ≤ 125 ms` per cue and `tail silence ≤ 500 ms` per block; otherwise the build fails with the offending cue id. This is the check none of TEA/PSA/Code2Video have (their judges score layout, not sync).

### A.3 What did not work

- Whisper/WhisperX word times as the sole source: 54–82 % within tolerance and multi-second outliers on spontaneous speech ([MFA 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18466), [Rousso 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19363v1)).
- manim-voiceover's char-offset interpolation when the transcript differs from the input (numbers, symbols, LaTeX read-outs): the scale factor `transcribed_len/net_text_len` is global, so one mis-transcribed word shifts every later bookmark. Coqui bookmarks were broken outright (#42).
- Gemini-TTS and OpenAI TTS have no timing output; Kokoro's timestamps are English-only; Azure's can return zeros.
- Block-level `run_time=tracker.duration` (TEA): sentence-level sync only, and TEA's own Kokoro service discards word boundaries, so bookmarks cannot be used.
- Letting an LLM emit `start_time`/`duration` for effects ([Script-to-Slide](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16931)) — grounding F1 0.924, but no timing accuracy is reported at all.

### A.4 Open questions

- No public ms-level numbers for Hindi/Hinglish aligners: [Pandey, Gogoi & Tang 2020](https://slam.phil.uni-duesseldorf.de/publication/pandey-2020-understanding/) ran MFA on a 7.4 h / 113-speaker code-mixed corpus and found neighbouring-phone context the main error driver, but the PDF was unreachable; [parseh/aligner-hi](https://huggingface.co/parseh/aligner-hi) (indicwav2vec CTC) reports only synthetic-speech agreement and "no accuracy guarantee". We must benchmark on our own TTS audio.
- The 250 ms lead is derived, not measured: the only direct evidence is that cues ±2.6 s off narration hurt ([Journal of Psychological Science 2017](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2017/V40/I5/1104)) and that "all cues at once" hurts ([Boucheix & Lowe 2010](https://lead.ube.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/000191-an-eye-tracking-comparison-of-external-pointing-cues-and-internal-continuous-cues-in-learning-with-complex-animations.pdf)).
- Do forced aligners behave better on clean TTS audio than on the human benchmarks? Likely, but unverified for MMS_FA on Hinglish.
- Whether Gemini pause markup produces stable, alignable silences.

### B.2 Adopt first

**1. One verbally-evoked focal cue, dim the rest (keep ours, tighten the numbers).**
The evidence supports exactly our pattern: colour/box on the named item at the moment it is named (Jamet; JPS 2017), everything else recedes (FocusOn idiom), no second cue type stacked on it (Frontiers 2026 negative combined effect). Design:

- Exactly one `focus` target per cue. Highlight = colour change of the target to the accent + `SurroundingRectangle(buff=SMALL_BUFF, stroke_width=3)` faded in over 0.25 s; non-targets to opacity **0.35** (3b1b's `FocusOn` uses 0.2; ours is 0.42 — pick inside that range and test readability of dimmed LaTeX).
- Highlight persists for the whole cue span, minimum **1.4 s** (JPS cue length), and hands over to the next cue with a 0.25 s cross-fade; never flashes.
- Transient effects (`Indicate`, `FlashAround`) are reserved for a referent that is *already* the focus and is named again; cap at one transient per 2 s.

**2. Automatic cue grounding against a registered mobject inventory with a validator.**
Script-to-Slide and MINARD show an LLM can map sentences to object ids reliably *when the candidate set is closed*. Design:

- Scene builder registers every teachable part: `reg("eq.lhs", mobj, desc="left-hand side 2x+3")`. Export inventory `{id, desc, bbox, parent}` per block.
- Planner prompt gets the narration sentence with its `<cue>` spans plus the inventory and must return `{cue_id → [target_id], effect ∈ {focus, reveal, trace}}`. Validator: unknown ids rejected; more than one `focus` per cue rejected; a `reveal` target must not already be visible.
- Fallback order mirrors CVIT: exact string match on `desc` (precise, low recall) → LLM semantic match → human-flag. Log grounding precision on a hand-labelled set; target ≥ 0.9 F1 before trusting it unattended.

**3. Progressive disclosure and path cues from the cue table.**
Boucheix & Lowe's result is that cues should *arrive with the event*, and "all at once" loses the effect. Design: default effect for a first mention is `reveal` (Write/FadeIn at `t_on − 250 ms`); for a multi-part referent spoken in order (steps of a derivation, nodes of a graph) use `trace`: `ShowPassingFlash`/sequential `set_color` across sub-mobjects at the per-word onsets; arrows only when direction is the content.

### B.3 What did not work

- Dynamic arrows as the primary cue: worse than colour propagation ([Boucheix & Lowe 2010](https://lead.ube.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/000191-an-eye-tracking-comparison-of-external-pointing-cues-and-internal-continuous-cues-in-learning-with-complex-animations.pdf)).
- Showing all cues at once, or cues ±2.6 s off narration.
- Magnification and content removal as highlight styles (CVIT: least liked, context loss).
- Stacking cue types (combined cues g = −0.64).
- Pure string matching for grounding (misses 66 % of references) and unconstrained VLM grounding (MINARD cites "broad over-highlighting").
- Investing in novel effects: the meta-analyses find cue *type* rarely moderates the effect; timing and target correctness do.

### B.4 Open questions

- Optimal dim level (0.2 vs 0.35 vs 0.42) and whether dimming hurts re-reading of earlier derivation steps; no study isolates this.
- Persisting vs transient highlights over long derivations: studies use short diagrams.
- Grounding accuracy on Manim inventories (equations with sub-parts) is unmeasured; Script-to-Slide's 0.924 is on text-only slides.
- Whether on-screen caption karaoke (Code2Video) adds or competes with the diagram cue; the combined-cue penalty suggests testing before adding.
- Hindi/Hinglish: cue words may be split across code-switch boundaries; the aligner's word onsets must survive romanization.
